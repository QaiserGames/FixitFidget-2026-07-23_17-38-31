## 4. What the Producer found in the code (the causes) — verify before you build on any of it

- **Carried parts float at a fixed depth.** `ReplaceablePart.Follow` holds a pinched part on the cursor's ray at the depth it was picked up at, 4 cm toward the camera (`HeldLift`); on screen it looks over the tray, in 3D it can be above or behind the device; let go, it falls straight down from where it really is. Released parts are rigidbodies (`ContinuousDynamic`, mass 0.01); thin parts at the Mac's 15–20 fps can tunnel; `BenchStage` has a catch that rescues anything that falls 25 cm under the mat (it happens). The fresh part seats only within `seatDistance` (Grace's camera: 0.016 m) of the seat, measured after sliding the held point along its sight line.
- **Three scales at one bench.** `PhoneRepair` is built at real size (70 × 140 mm); Grace's camera at 2× (`hero_camera.py`: "at the prefab's 2x size": 240 × 160 × 46 mm body, 33 cm with the strap); the pocket watch at 3× (`hero_watch.py`: a 150 mm case). Tools are real size (`bench_tools.py`); the tray is 17 × 17 cm outside (`Playtest3Session4Steps.cs`); the mat 60 × 36 cm, a plain slab "until the devices session models them" — never modelled.
- **Enormous holes.** The phone's screws are 8 mm heads ("a cartoon size kept so it can be hit from 50 cm; a real one is 1.2 mm", `hero_phone.py`); the watch's 12 mm. The hole a screw leaves (`ScrewSocket.Make` in `Screw.cs`) is drawn at the head's full width: a countersink disc the head's diameter and a near-black core at 0.9 × the radius. On a real-size phone that is an 8 mm black pit.
- **The cloth and the brush.** The cloth model is a rigid folded block (75 × 90 mm, `bench_tools.py`); in the hand it is a 34 × 41 mm pad trailing the cursor on a spring (`ToolPickup`, now closed-form), lifting off after each smudge. You pick a tool by clicking it, then hold Use over the glass. Clean width is fixed in metres (`GrimeSpot`: `BrushRadius .0048`, `ClothRadius .0095`, "real-sized whatever the device's scale"), so on the 2× camera and 3× watch it works at a half and a third; a spot counts clean at 97 %.
- **The "handle spring thingy"** is almost certainly the camera's **jammed shutter mechanism** (`hero_camera.py` `mechanism()`: a chassis, a shutter drum, three brass gears, a cocking lever, a coiled spring, three chassis screws) — the broken part he must pinch, lift and drop, then pick the fresh part (one steel petal, `blade(False)`) from the tray and drop within 1.6 cm of its seat. The fresh part looks nothing like what came out.
- **The camera's "handle"** is the strap: flat chamfered slabs (two 83 × 17 mm bands with a slide buckle, a 140 × 18 mm worn loop), lying straight. The scene names it "KEEP - scratched sentimental strap": Grace's piece.
- **The checklist** (`BenchCards`, `RepairChecklist`): a 760 px strip top-middle plus the controls card bottom-right plus the Day 1 card top-left plus the ticket plus the prompt line: about 120 words on the first repair's screen (the First Hour playtester counted them).
- **Sound:** the whole game has **two** real sound files (`Assets/Audio/Dispenser/CupReady.wav`, `SoftPour.wav`). Every other cue — screws, tray tinks, brush, cloth, the bell, the intro's sting — is synthesized in code (`PlaceholderSounds.cs`). Cues are looked up by name in the `SoundBank`; a real file for a cue replaces the placeholder with no code change.
- **The intro** (`DayIntro.cs`): four slots (the café → the bench → a third → the open) with two or three shots each; "a morning Grace is coming early shows her house" (Day 2 shows a white, empty room through her window); the grinder close-up has no beans modelled; the tray close-up is a near-empty tray.
- **The night zoom** (`CafeViewMode.cs`): round the back of the café `Lodger.BinsView` calls `EnterHouseView` (house limits 8–22 m); walking away it calls `ExitHouseView`, which starts an eased `TurnTo` back to the outside limits (24–48 m); **any camera input sets `turning = false`**, cancelling the ease and leaving `isoDistance` below the new minimum, and nothing re-clamps until a zoom input, when `Mathf.Clamp(…, DistanceMin, DistanceMax)` jumps it to 24 m in one frame.
- **The stools:** the playtesters saw two of the three repair-bar stools never used (cause unknown); there is no "sit on a high stool" clip (bar stools have Snap To Seat off; the perch clip is in Mixamo batch 2, his download, not done).
- **The café from above:** the 13 lived-in props read in first person and almost vanish from the overhead camera (the First Hour's before/after photos). Day 1 has at most two or three customers by design.

## 5. What ReStory (and three others) actually do — the bar, from sources

*ReStory: Chill Electronics Repairs* (Mandragora / tinyBuild, 6 Aug 2026, $19.99; 300k+ copies in its first week; 95 % positive of 5,600+ Steam reviews; OpenCritic 89):
- **One repeatable structure:** unscrew the casing → remove components layer by layer → inspect: dirty parts need cleaning, broken ones replacing → dirty parts on one side of the bench, clean on the other → brush every dirty part → source replacements → reassemble in reverse → "Complete and send". A **notepad on the right** tracks which parts are dirty, broken or missing and the order they go back in; "if you have extra parts left after assembly, you skipped a step" (Pro Game Guides; VGTimes).
- **Screws** are loosened "and placed in a nearby receptacle (just as you would do in real life), and slotting in pieces always rewards you with the appropriate (and satisfying) audio cues" (Automaton). Assemble with Care's best-loved moment is the same: unscrewing a screw and dropping it into a bowl (iMore).
- **Forgiving on purpose:** COGconnected's reviewer: "I could yank a tiny screw straight out of a cramped, tucked-away corner even while it faced away from him. No fumbling required." **No clock:** "A day only ends when the player decides to end it."
- **Light on screen:** "doesn't rely too heavily on UI and HUDs … The checklist pops up as you work on a new project"; "satisfying sound effects such as clicks and squeaks" (KeenGamer).
- **Upgrades make the slow parts faster:** the brush's radius ("clean larger areas with each stroke"); the screwdriver's second upgrade makes screws instant.
- **Its own weak spots:** rotation "slides past the angle I need or snaps back"; "Cursor controls cause strain" (Vaporlens); critics want "clearer indication of screwed/unscrewed screws" and to "highlight parts hidden under other parts" (KeenGamer); "some of the screws we have to take out are a little small" and getting lost in reassembly (SteamDeckHQ).
- **PowerWash Simulator:** a **Dirt Indicator** button (Tab on PC, D-pad right on pads) "will show even the tiniest specks of filth that have yet to be removed" (Pro Game Guides); dirt particles take the dirt's colour (FuturLab, Xbox Wire); no time limits in the core game.
- **Unpacking:** 14,000+ foley files recorded from the real objects by one family; "Pick-up sounds are short and punchy" because slower ones felt laggy; a dynamic shake system follows mouse speed (Game Developer, GDC 2022).
- **Assemble with Care:** the first job is a cassette respooled with a pencil — the easiest thing in the game first (iMore).

Sources: progameguides.com (ReStory beginner's guide), vgtimes.com (ReStory guide), cogconnected.com (ReStory review), keengamer.com (ReStory preview), automaton-media.com (demo impressions), steamdeckhq.com (preview), vaporlens.app (review summary), store.steampowered.com/app/3812600, nettosgameroom.com and 80.lv (sales), imore.com (Assemble with Care review), progameguides.com (PowerWash tips), news.xbox.com (PowerWash 2), gamedeveloper.com (Unpacking audio).

## 6. His decisions (14 Oct, 05:59): "for all your questions, i trust your judgement to do the best option, the option that is closest to my request."

The Producer took the option closest to his request on every call. These are now the plan; each is his to overrule.

1. **The auto-tray.** Taking apart stays physical and under your hand (the screw backs out of the hole you clicked along its own axis; the cover pops where you pried; the tweezers pinch). Everything after that is automatic: each freed piece flies home on a scripted arc — screws into the screw cups, covers and loose parts into the parts well, broken parts into the old-parts bin — and lands with its sound. Nothing lies loose on the mat; nothing can fall under the device or through the table.
2. **The two-hold swap.** Hold the tweezers on the broken part: it lifts out with a wobble and flies to the old-parts bin. Click the fresh part (waiting in its marked spot): it flies to its seat and hovers; hold, and it presses in with a snap. No carrying, no dropping, no depth.
3. **One scale at the bench:** devices, screws, holes, tools, tray and mat in real proportion to each other, with the close-up framing each device to fill the mat. The Technical Director writes the method (what changes, what breaks, how it's proven) before anything moves.
4. **Tools by direct grab:** point at the cloth or brush and press — it is in your hand under the cursor with no lag; drag across the surface — it presses down, the cloth bunches toward the stroke, the clean trail appears under it; let go — it goes back to its place. "Feel the impact" = the look (press and bunch), the sound and rumble (a rub that follows speed, a pad rumble), and the device budging a few millimetres; a gleam and a chime when clean. Spots clear in two to four strokes; once 90 % is gone the next pass finishes it; a **show-dirt** button flashes what's left.
5. **Screws and holes in real proportion, with big invisible hit areas** (visuals real, targets generous, aim help on top). The hole is a small threaded opening in a slight dip, not a dark disc. Hover prefers the part the current step needs.
6. **The mat:** a silicone phone-repair mat (the kind repair techs use: a printed grid, rulers along the edges, moulded screw cups and a parts well), in a **cool slate blue** so the warm devices pop against it (value and hue contrast). The tray's job moves into the mat's moulded compartments plus an old-parts bin at its edge; a tool stand replaces the plain caddy block.
7. **The checklist:** one small paper-styled card **on screen** (the "objects are objects, information is projected" lock stays): the customer's name and what's wrong, then the job as three to six tool icons that tick off, the current one bigger; the next thing to touch glows softly on the device (colour and shape in the world: allowed). The controls card on the first repair only, then behind a button. **Under 25 words on the bench screen.**
8. **Day 1 (the intro level):** Grace brings the camera on Day 1 for **a lens and film-path clean only** (cloth and brush, the most forgiving verbs); she comes back on **Day 2** for the shutter (the two-hold swap, now forgiving) — a return beat for her arc. Her Day 1 latte suppression stays (locked). Walk-ins on Day 1 stay light; the Narrative and Progression hats decide the rest of Days 1–3, **including the day's length** (180 s today).
9. **The café full from above:** settled guests on Day 1 who never need you (reading, laptops, chatting), and repair-café props dressed for the overhead camera (clusters, colour, height), plus a couple of props that tell Ace's story (a mortgage letter, a final notice). ## 7. The studio's rules from today (binding)

1. **Reference before work.** A lead whose task changes how the game feels studies real footage or sources first (the reference agent's feel sheet lands at `~/cafe/feel/feel-sheet-2026-10-14.md` during this wave; read it if it exists when you finish, and say where your spec disagrees with it).
2. **Feel targets before building** (the Producer writes them from the feel sheet). Provisional targets for this wave: a first-timer finishes Day 1's first repair in under three minutes; the bench screen carries under 25 words; every action makes a sound the instant it happens; nothing removed can end up out of sight; a dirt spot clears in two to four strokes; no action needs a precise drop.
3. **A clumsy hand in the labs.** The bench lab gets a cursor that moves at human speed with overshoot, jitter, near-misses and slightly-off releases, and reports time and retries. If the clumsy hand can't finish Day 1's and Day 2's repairs inside their budget, the build is not ready, whatever else is green.
4. **The playtesters stop grading feel** from photos; they report risks for his hands and analyse his recordings.
5. **His playtests are recorded,** and the agents timestamp every struggle.
6. **Nobody helps a playtester** while they play.
7. **No physics in the core loop.** Scripted motion for everything the player must rely on; physics only as flourish that can never lose a piece.
8. **Labs prove what he would see, at his frame rate** (15–20 fps on the Mac with Unity in the background), not a proxy.
