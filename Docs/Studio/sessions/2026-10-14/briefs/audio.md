## The job
1. **The cue table** for the bench and its neighbours, in the forgiving bench's terms (common brief §6: screws fly to moulded cups in a silicone mat; covers to a parts well; broken parts to an old-parts bin; the fresh part flies to its seat and presses in; cloth and brush grabbed directly, rubs follow stroke speed): cue name (existing names where they exist) · where it fires · what it must tell the player · length · layering · priority/voice limit · how it varies (pitch/volume jitter, by speed) · status today. Lessons to apply: Unpacking — action sounds short and punchy (slow pick-ups felt laggy), sounds follow the mouse's speed; ReStory — "satisfying audio cues" on every slot-in; a tink for every screw in the receptacle. Include: driver ticks, the free click, the flight, the cup tink (metal into silicone vs metal into metal: pick), the seat snap; the pry creak and pop, the cover's lift and its land in the well, its snap home; the pinch, the lift, the part into the old-parts bin, the press-in snap; the brush (a loop driven by stroke speed) and its dust; the cloth (a microfibre rub loop driven by speed, a glass squeak on direction changes), the gleam chime; "Fixed!"; each device's wake (the phone's chime, the watch's ticking, the camera's shutter); tool grab and return; the checklist's tick. Then the café's room tone, the door bell, the till, the intro's sting.
2. **Get real files now, legally.** Test from this cloud workspace what you can actually reach (`curl -sI` on candidate hosts; GitHub and package registries usually work, most other sites don't). Look for **CC0** sources first (e.g. Kenney's audio packs, OpenGameArt CC0 packs, any CC0 collections mirrored on GitHub — verify the licence file in the source itself), then permissive licences that allow commercial games (record the attribution text if needed). Never use anything whose licence you can't see. Download into `~/cafe/audio/src/<source>/`, keep the licence files beside them. If you get usable files: pick, trim, fade, normalise (ffmpeg/sox are available or installable), mono, 44.1 kHz, name them per cue into `~/cafe/audio/out/` with a `manifest.json` (cue → file → source → licence → volume). **Plan C, if nothing reachable fits:** render better procedural sounds offline in Python (modal synthesis for metal: a few inharmonic damped partials plus a short noise transient; filtered noise loops for rubs) — say honestly how they compare to recordings.
3. **His recording list.** A one-page shot list Mansoor can do with his phone in 20 minutes: each sound, the real object, how to make it (distance, surface, 3 takes, quiet room, phone's voice-memo app or a recorder app, no music), what to name the file, and where to drop the files (`~/Documents/FixitFidget/Recordings/` on the Mac; the Producer imports them).
4. **The import and implementation steps**: how files go into the `SoundBank` (by cue name), import settings, the mixer bus, and **the code hooks needed** for speed-following loops (what number from `GrimeSpot`/the tool drives volume and pitch) — specify; Systems will build them.

Output: `~/cafe/specs/audio-bench-sounds.md` (plus the files under `~/cafe/audio/` if you got any). Your final message is the spec, with a list of the files you produced.
