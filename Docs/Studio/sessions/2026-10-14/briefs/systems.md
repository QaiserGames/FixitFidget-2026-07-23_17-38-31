## The job: the mechanism spec for decisions 1, 2, 4 and 5 of the common brief, plus the clumsy-hand lab
Assume the Technical Director's "one scale" lands (devices, screws, tools, mat in real proportion; write every distance **relative to the device or in screen pixels**, never in fixed metres that assume today's 2×/3× devices). Assume Art Direction's mat layout: **moulded screw cups, a parts well, an old-parts bin at the mat's edge, and a marked spot where a fresh part waits** — positions are data on `BenchStage` (name the fields you need).

Write, verb by verb, **what he will see** and then **the exact mechanism**:
1. **Screws.** Keep the hold-to-unscrew turn along the screw's own axis (he liked the driver). On free: the tiny pop, then a scripted flight to its cup (path, duration, arc height, spin, easing, landing cue, which cup — one per screw in removal order, or grouped), never a rigidbody. Back in: hold on the empty hole → the screw flies from its cup to hover over the hole → the hold seats it (today's reversed turn and snap). What happens to `BenchStage`'s magnet and catch (likely obsolete for screws).
2. **Covers.** The pry hold stays physical (lever at the seam, creak, pop). Then: does the cover lift off and fly to the parts well by itself after the pop, or on one more click? Pick, with reasoning (fewest inputs that still feel physical). Putting it back: click the cover in the well (or hold on the open seat) → it flies back and snaps.
3. **The two-hold swap.** Hold the tweezers on the broken part → it lifts with today's wobble → it flies to the old-parts bin (on release, or after a lift time: pick). Click the fresh part in its marked spot → it flies to its seat and hovers, aligned → hold (pick the duration) → it presses in with a snap. Delete the carry-at-depth (`ReplaceablePart.Follow`, `HeldLift`, `seatDistance`, `SeatWouldTake` and `AtDepthOf` as a gate) from the player's path; say what the labs' hand (`Activate`) does now.
4. **Cloth and brush by direct grab.** Press on the tool in its stand → it is in the hand under the cursor with zero lag (no spring for the position; a little tilt/bunch lag is fine as a look) → drag across a surface → the tool presses down, the clean trail appears under it → let go → it returns to its stand. Keep a pad path (LB/RB still choose a tool; hold RT/A to press it down). The look of "pressing" (bunch toward the stroke for the cloth; bristles bending for the brush), the device budging a few millimetres under the stroke (spring back), the rub feedback (a cue whose volume/pitch follows stroke speed: say exactly what number drives it, so Audio can make the loop), pad rumble. **Clean width relative to the dirt spot** so a spot clears in two to four ordinary strokes; **at 90 % gone, the next stroke over it finishes it**; grease "smears first, then clears" (say how, in mask terms). Today's cloth lift-off after a smudge (playtest 4) — keep or drop, with reasoning.
5. **Show dirt.** One button flashes what's left for about 1.5 s: remaining dirt texels pulse, and parts with work left glow. UI/UX picks the binding and the look; you specify the mechanism and its data.
6. **Hit areas and hover.** Visuals real-proportion, targets generous: a rule in **screen pixels** at 1080p (e.g. any bench target is hit within N px of its centre), hover priority (the current checklist step's part first, then the nearest), what aim help does on a pad now, how hidden parts (under others) are reached.
7. **Nothing is ever lost or hidden:** list every way a piece could end up out of sight or unreachable today and how the new design makes each impossible.
8. **Time budget.** Estimate first-timer hands-on time for: Grace's camera clean (Day 1: lens smudge + film path + film guide), Grace's shutter swap (Day 2), the phone's cracked screen, the watch's seized movement. Show the arithmetic (inputs × seconds). The Days 1–3 lead uses these numbers for the day length.

**The clumsy-hand lab** (rule 3): specify a cursor model that plays like a person (reaction delay; Fitts's-law movement time; overshoot with a corrective sub-movement; jitter; a share of near-miss clicks; releases slightly off; occasionally the wrong tool first), running the real input path (mouse and pad), at 60 fps and at 15 fps. What it plays (the four repairs above), what it reports (time per step, retries, misses, pieces lost (must be 0), total vs budget), its pass bars, where it lives (a new lab beside Bench 3; menu path), and which existing labs/checks must change (Bench 3, Bench 5, Stations, Aim help, Day 1 guide, Juice) and how.
