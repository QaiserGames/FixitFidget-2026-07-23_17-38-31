## Patterns
- **Plants built from spheres are the street's worst programmer art:** the sidewalk trees, the patio flower, the car park's sage balls (and the pocket sage, now hidden). The Kenney swap fixed the courtyard and stopped there. One more pass of the same swap closes the family. Rule: never a sphere for a plant.
- **Boxes suit hard things; soft things are what read wrong.** Houses, shopfronts, plaques, blades, gates and bollards built from boxes read fine. Boxes standing in for soft things (the coats; the coat stand's scarf and tote, the throw, the coffee bags; Barnaby's capsule placeholder) are what look wrong, and all but the coats are already off.
- **Replacement passes stop at a group's border, so leftovers survive.** Examples: the parts tray (floor off, walls on); the sphere trees (courtyard done, sidewalk missed); the patio (chairs swapped, flower kept). When a pass replaces a family, search the whole scene for it by material and shape, not by group.
- **New camera angles show faces nobody built.** The dumpster lid's underside, the brick blocks' blank sides, the box houses' blank side walls, the uphill ramps: the intro's pull-outs, first person and opened lids show what the overhead never did.
- **Bare glowing primitives work as lights from above, not up close.** The porch-lamp sphere, the cube lanterns, the bulb discs and the works lamps read as points of light overhead and as primitives up close. Give a fixture to the ones first person can reach.
- **The street paints are multiplied by a plaster photo texture,** which dulls and darkens them; Grace's saffron reads brown. Where a colour is a cue, keep it flat and saturated.
- Also seen, not assets: the conversation camera above the ceiling (K:Night__night-two-check-cups/13, 15 Oct); the first-person "You" pin (P:021712/17a, plan note 3); a mid-fade frame (K:DayIntro__check/6-skipped-first-frame).
